Web design for healthcare operates under a specific constraint that most other industries do not face: patients are researching you at a moment of vulnerability. Whether they are looking for a GP, a specialist, a dental practice, or a private clinic in the US, UK, Canada, or Australia, they are trusting you with their health. Your website must communicate clinical credibility, professional standards, and genuine care — and it must do so compliantly, within the regulatory framework of your jurisdiction.

This guide covers the design standards, required pages, compliance considerations, and trust signals that make healthcare websites convert patients while maintaining professional standards.

---

## What Do Patients Look for on a Healthcare Website?

Research by Healthgrades found that 77% of patients search online before booking a healthcare appointment. Of those, they primarily look for:

1. **Credentials and qualifications** — Is this practitioner or clinic genuinely qualified?
2. **Services offered** — Does this clinic treat my specific condition or provide the service I need?
3. **Location and accessibility** — How far is it, and how do I get there?
4. **How to book** — Can I book online, or do I have to call?
5. **Reviews** — What do other patients say?

Healthcare websites that surface all five elements quickly and clearly consistently outperform those that bury credentials in a "About" page that requires three clicks to find.

## What Pages Does a Healthcare Website Need?

**For GP surgeries and general practice clinics:**
- Homepage with location, hours, and appointment booking CTA
- Services page (list of conditions treated and services offered)
- Meet the team (credentials, GMC/AHPRA/medical board registration numbers)
- Patient information (appointment booking, referrals, test results)
- Contact and location with embedded map

**For specialists and private clinics:**
- Homepage with specialty positioning and appointment CTA
- Individual treatment/service pages (one page per treatment for SEO)
- Clinician profiles with full credentials, publications, and board certifications
- Patient journey page (what to expect before/during/after)
- Insurance and pricing information (for private/self-pay clinics)
- Testimonials and reviews (subject to jurisdiction rules)

**For healthcare technology companies:**
- Product/platform pages with clinical evidence
- Security and compliance page (HIPAA, NHS Data Security, GDPR)
- Integration documentation
- About and team (including clinical advisors)

## What Trust Signals Are Essential on Healthcare Websites?

Trust signals in healthcare are more formal and more important than in most other industries. Key elements:

**Credentials:** Medical registration numbers (GMC number in the UK, AHPRA registration in Australia, state medical board certification in the US) should appear on clinician profiles. For clinics, CQC registration (UK), ACHS accreditation (Australia), or Joint Commission accreditation (US) should be prominently displayed.

**Professional associations:** College memberships, Royal College fellowships, AMA membership, specialty board certifications — these signal peer validation that patients find highly credible.

**Patient reviews:** Google Reviews, Doctify (UK), Healthgrades (US), or RateMDs (Canada/Australia) — direct or aggregated review scores displayed on the homepage and clinician profiles increase booking rates significantly.

**Clinical photography:** Real photos of your clinic, your equipment, and your team. Not stock hospital photography that could come from anywhere. Patients researching a private clinic in Sydney or London want to see the actual environment they will visit.

This connects to the broader trust signal framework in [brand identity for healthcare](/blog/brand-identity-for-healthcare).

## What Compliance Requirements Affect Healthcare Web Design?

Healthcare website compliance varies by jurisdiction and practice type:

**USA (HIPAA):** Any online form that collects patient health information must be HIPAA-compliant. This includes contact forms that ask about conditions, appointment booking systems, and patient portals. Use HIPAA-compliant form tools (JotForm HIPAA, Typeform Healthcare, or a purpose-built patient management system).

**UK (CQC / NHS):** Private clinics registered with the Care Quality Commission must comply with CQC advertising standards. Claims about treatment outcomes must be evidence-based and not misleading. NHS information must clearly distinguish private from NHS services where both are offered.

**Australia (AHPRA):** The Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency prohibits certain types of testimonials and outcome claims in healthcare advertising. Patient testimonials that make claims about treatment effectiveness are specifically restricted.

**Canada (Provincial colleges):** Provincial medical colleges (CPSO in Ontario, CPSBC in BC) govern advertising standards. Patient testimonials and before/after imagery have specific rules that vary by province and specialty.

Always have your website copy and any claims reviewed by a healthcare compliance specialist before publishing. Design studios can advise on presentation; legal compliance must be verified by a qualified healthcare regulatory expert.

## What Are the Design Standards for Healthcare Websites?

**Colour:** Healthcare websites traditionally use blue (trust, calm, clinical), white (cleanliness, clarity), and green (health, healing). Warmer palettes — cream, sage, and warm white — work well for wellness-oriented practices (psychology, physiotherapy, wellness clinics) where warmth is as important as clinical credibility.

**Typography:** Clean, readable sans-serif typefaces at large sizes. Healthcare content is often complex — drug names, procedure descriptions, condition terminology. Typography that prioritises readability over design distinction serves patients better.

**Photography:** Real clinical photography significantly outperforms stock for credibility. Patients can immediately identify stock hospital imagery. If budget allows, photography of the actual clinic, actual equipment, and actual clinical team (in professional context) is the highest-ROI marketing investment most clinics can make.

**Accessibility:** Healthcare websites have a higher-than-average proportion of visitors with disabilities, cognitive challenges, or visual impairments. WCAG 2.1 AA compliance — adequate contrast ratios, alt text on all images, keyboard navigation — is both ethically required and legally important in most jurisdictions.

## How Should Online Appointment Booking Be Implemented?

Online appointment booking converts significantly better than phone-only booking for most patient demographics under 55. Implementation options:

**Proprietary practice management systems:** Most practice management software (Healthengine in AU, Doctify in UK, Zocdoc in US) includes online booking as a feature. Embedding this booking flow directly on your website (not redirecting to a third-party page) provides the best patient experience.

**Custom booking forms:** For practices that do not use a booking platform, a branded, accessible online form that collects patient name, contact, preferred appointment type, and insurance information — and sends confirmation immediately — is effective and compliant.

The booking form must be mobile-optimised. See [web design for professional services](/blog/web-design-for-professional-services) for general standards on professional services booking flows.

## What Makes Healthcare Web Design Different From Other Industries?

Three factors make healthcare web design uniquely demanding:

1. **The emotional state of the visitor:** Patients are often anxious, unwell, or seeking information about a frightening diagnosis. Design that reduces anxiety through clarity, warmth, and credibility serves patients and converts better.
2. **The complexity of the information:** Medical information must be accurate, appropriately simplified, and compliant with advertising regulations simultaneously.
3. **The trust threshold:** The cost of a wrong healthcare decision is severe. The trust threshold required before a patient books is higher than almost any other service category.

These constraints are why generic website templates perform so poorly for healthcare. The same template that works for a marketing agency fails for a cardiology practice because the emotional context and trust requirements are entirely different.
