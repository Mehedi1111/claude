Web design for law firms operates on a different set of rules than almost any other industry. When someone searches for a solicitor, attorney, or legal practice in the US, UK, Canada, or Australia, they are not browsing casually — they have a problem that may involve their family, their business, or their financial security. Your website has approximately 8 seconds to signal that you are the right firm to trust with that problem. If the design looks outdated, generic, or inconsistent, they leave. Credibility is the only metric that matters.

This guide covers what law firm websites must communicate, the design standards that build trust, and how to structure your site to convert visitors into enquiries.

---

## What Do Prospective Clients Look for on a Law Firm Website?

Prospective legal clients look for three things, in this order: **credibility** (are you a serious, established firm?), **relevance** (do you handle my specific problem?), and **approachability** (can I contact you without feeling intimidated?).

Research by Legal Marketing Association found that 74% of people who need legal help start their search online, and 96% of those visit a law firm's website before making contact. Of those, 59% say the website's design directly influenced whether they chose to enquire. Your website is your most important new business tool — more important than referrals for first-contact acquisition.

## What Pages Does a Law Firm Website Need?

A professional law firm website requires:

1. **Homepage** — Positioning statement, practice areas, credibility signals (awards, bar admissions, years in practice), clear call to action
2. **Practice areas** — Individual pages for each practice area with specific content about the legal issues you handle
3. **Attorney/solicitor profiles** — Individual pages for each lawyer with professional photo, credentials, experience, and bar admissions
4. **About the firm** — History, values, and what distinguishes your approach
5. **Results/case studies** — Where ethically and legally permitted, showcase outcomes
6. **Client testimonials** — Reviews and testimonials (subject to jurisdiction ethics rules)
7. **Contact page** — Multiple contact methods, office address, map, response time expectation
8. **Blog/resources** — Legal content marketing that demonstrates expertise

Individual practice area pages are particularly important for SEO. A page titled "Commercial Litigation — New York" will rank for specific searches that your generic homepage never will.

## What Design Standards Apply to Law Firm Websites?

**Colour:** Law firm websites traditionally use dark navy, charcoal, deep burgundy, or forest green as primary colours — colours that communicate authority, stability, and gravitas. Bright, playful palettes are not appropriate for most legal practices. If your firm has a specialisation (family law, immigration, employment rights) you may have slightly more latitude, but the baseline is professional and restrained.

**Typography:** Serif typefaces (Georgia, Garamond, Tiempos) communicate tradition and authority — appropriate for established firms. Humanist sans-serifs (Inter, Source Sans) communicate accessibility and modernity — appropriate for newer firms positioning as more approachable. Never use decorative or display fonts for body content.

**Photography:** Professional headshots of your lawyers are non-negotiable. Generic stock photography of gavels, scales, and courthouse steps is immediately recognisable as low-investment and damages credibility. Invest in real photography of your team, your office, and ideally your courtroom or professional context. See the [brand photography guide](/blog/brand-photography-guide) for standards.

**White space:** Legal content is dense. Generous white space, clear paragraph breaks, and readable line lengths (60–80 characters per line) significantly reduce the friction of reading complex content.

## How Should a Law Firm Website Handle Practice Areas?

Each practice area should have its own dedicated page — not a section on a single "Services" page. This matters for three reasons:

1. **SEO:** A page titled "Employment Discrimination Attorney — Chicago" can rank for that specific search. A generic "Practice Areas" page cannot.
2. **Client relevance:** A client with a specific legal problem wants to see that you genuinely specialise in their area, not that you handle "everything."
3. **Content depth:** Each practice area allows you to explain the specific legal issues, typical process, and outcomes for that type of work — building confidence and answering the questions clients have before they call.

Structure each practice area page with: a clear description of the issue, who it affects, how your firm approaches it, what the client can expect, and a direct call to action. ## What Trust Signals Must Appear on a Law Firm Website?

Trust signals convert visitors into enquiries. The most effective for law firm websites:

- **Bar admissions and memberships** — State Bar (US), Law Society (UK/AU), Law Society of Upper Canada — prominently displayed
- **Years in practice** — Established 1998, 25+ years of combined experience
- **Case results** — Where permitted, specific outcomes ("Secured $2.4M settlement in commercial dispute")
- **Awards and recognition** — Best Lawyers, Chambers and Partners, Legal 500, Super Lawyers
- **Client testimonials** — Subject to jurisdiction rules, real testimonials from named (or anonymised) clients
- **Professional photo of the lead attorney** — Visible above the fold on the homepage

Research by Clio found that law firms displaying trust signals prominently on their homepage saw 34% higher enquiry rates than those that did not. This mirrors the [social proof strategy](/blog/social-proof-brand-strategy) used by all high-converting professional service websites.

## Should a Law Firm Use a Contact Form or Phone Number?

Both — but the hierarchy matters. Phone numbers should be visible in the header on every page (particularly important for mobile users in urgent situations). A contact form provides a lower-friction option for clients who are not yet ready to call.

Do not gate your phone number behind a form. Legal enquiries are often time-sensitive, and any friction at the contact stage loses clients to a competitor whose number is immediately visible.

For the contact form, collect: name, email, phone, preferred contact method, brief description of the matter, and preferred office location (if you have multiple). Confirm receipt immediately with an automated response that sets expectations for response time (typically 1 business day).

## How Does Law Firm Web Design Differ From Other Professional Services?

Law firm websites must balance two competing pressures that most professional services websites do not face: **regulatory constraints** (ethics rules around advertising, testimonials, and outcome claims vary by jurisdiction) and **the urgency of the enquiry** (many legal problems are time-sensitive, and the client is researching multiple firms simultaneously).

The design implication: law firm websites need to move prospective clients from discovery to contact faster than most professional services websites. The call to action should appear on every page, multiple times, and should be frictionless. "Call us now" with a phone number should appear in the header, mid-page, and footer on every single page.

Compare this to a management consultant website, which typically has longer consideration cycles and can afford more content-heavy journeys before a contact CTA. See [web design for consultants](/blog/web-design-for-consultants) for the contrast.

## What Technology Should a Law Firm Website Be Built On?

For most law firms of 1–20 attorneys in the US, UK, Canada, or Australia, the right technology stack is:

- **Next.js + Vercel:** Best performance, best SEO capabilities, fully custom design. Ideal for firms that want a distinctive, brand-aligned website with maximum search visibility.
- **WordPress + Elementor/Divi:** Easier for internal content updates. Good option for firms that need to publish legal guides frequently without developer help.
- **Clio Grow (legal-specific):** Purpose-built for law firms with CRM integration. Useful if you need intake forms and client management connected to your website.

Whatever technology you choose, the site must be fast (under 2.5 seconds load time), mobile-optimised (over 60% of legal searches happen on mobile), and HTTPS-secured. See [how much does web design cost](/blog/how-much-does-web-design-cost) for budget expectations.
