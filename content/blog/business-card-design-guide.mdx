Business card design remains one of the most underestimated brand investments a professional service firm can make. In the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, 72% of people judge a company's quality based on the physical materials they receive — and your business card is often the first physical object a prospective client takes away from a meeting. A card that feels cheap, looks generic, or fails to reflect your brand positioning costs you credibility before the conversation is even over.

This guide covers what your business card communicates, what specifications matter, and how to design a card that reinforces — rather than undermines — your brand.

---

## What Does Your Business Card Communicate?

Your business card communicates four things before anyone reads a single word: quality, confidence, attention to detail, and positioning. The weight of the card stock, the finish, the white space, and the precision of the typography all signal what kind of firm you are. A 400gsm matte card with a spot UV logo says something entirely different from a 300gsm gloss card from an online template builder.

In professional services — consulting, law, finance, architecture — the average deal size is $10,000–$250,000. A $0.80 premium business card versus a $0.10 generic one is not a meaningful cost relative to a single closed deal. The question is not whether to invest in quality; it's whether your card is doing active work for your brand.

## What Are the Standard Business Card Dimensions in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia?

Standard business card sizes vary slightly by market:

- **USA and Canada:** 3.5 × 2 inches (88.9 × 50.8 mm)
- **UK:** 85 × 55 mm (slightly taller than US)
- **Australia:** 90 × 55 mm (most common; some use 90 × 54 mm)

Design files should include a 3mm bleed on all sides and be supplied at 300 DPI minimum. Your logo file must be vector or high-resolution raster at print size — never scale up a low-resolution logo. See our [logo file formats guide](/blog/logo-file-formats-explained) for the exact specifications to provide your printer.

## What Information Should Go on a Business Card?

Less is more. The most effective business cards in 2026 include:

1. **Your name** (prominent)
2. **Your role or title**
3. **Company name** (or logo — not both if redundant)
4. **Primary email**
5. **Phone number** (mobile preferred)
6. **Website URL**
7. **LinkedIn profile** (optional but valuable for B2B)

Do not include your full address unless clients visit your office. Do not include social media handles unless they are genuinely active. A card crammed with contact methods signals insecurity; a card with the right five details signals clarity.

## What Card Stock and Finish Options Build the Strongest Brand Impression?

Card stock and finish are where business card design diverges sharply in brand perception:

**Card stock weight:**
- **300–350gsm:** Standard quality. Acceptable but forgettable.
- **400–600gsm:** Premium feel. Immediately signals investment and quality.
- **Duplex (two layers bonded):** Exceptionally thick. Used by luxury and premium firms.

**Finish options:**
- **Matte laminate:** Clean, modern, professional. Works for most B2B service firms.
- **Soft-touch matte:** Velvet-like feel. Extremely premium. Popular with consulting and finance.
- **Spot UV:** Gloss varnish applied to specific elements (logo, name). High contrast effect.
- **Foil stamping:** Gold, silver, or custom foil on selected elements. Luxury positioning signal.
- **Edge painting:** Colored edges on thick cards. Bold and memorable.

For professional services firms targeting enterprise clients in New York, London, Toronto, or Sydney, soft-touch matte at 400gsm+ is the minimum standard worth considering. Your [brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-what-they-include) should specify approved finishes alongside colour values.

## Should Your Business Card Be Single-Sided or Double-Sided?

Both work — the choice depends on your purpose. Single-sided cards are cleaner and give the recipient somewhere to write notes. Double-sided cards give you space for a tagline, a core service statement, or a QR code linking to your portfolio or booking page.

If you use the back, make it purposeful. A tagline, a single differentiating statement ("Brand strategy for B2B firms ready to charge more"), or a QR code to a relevant landing page are all strong choices. A full-colour design on the back signals investment. A blank back on premium stock signals confident restraint.

## How Should Your Logo Appear on a Business Card?

Your logo should appear once, clearly, and at an appropriate size. It should not be oversized, stretched, or placed without adequate breathing room. The minimum clear space rule from your [brand identity guidelines](/blog/visual-identity-guide) applies on business cards just as it does on digital applications.

If your logo contains a wordmark and a symbol, consider using just the symbol on cards where your name is already printed separately — this creates a cleaner hierarchy. If your firm is less well-known, keep the full wordmark visible for recognition purposes. See our [logo file formats guide](/blog/logo-file-formats-explained) to ensure you have the right file type for your printer.

## What Typography Rules Apply to Business Card Design?

Business card typography follows three rules: legibility, hierarchy, and restraint.

- **Minimum body text size:** 7pt at print. 8pt is safer for names and contact details.
- **Name:** Should be the largest text element (10–14pt depending on length).
- **Supporting text:** 7–9pt, lighter weight, sufficient contrast with background.
- **Maximum font families:** Two. One from your brand identity system, one complementary if needed.

Do not use script or display fonts for contact information. Do not place light text on a pale background — contrast must be high enough to read under all lighting conditions. Your [brand colours guide](/blog/brand-colors-guide) should define approved background and text pairings.

## How Much Should Good Business Cards Cost?

For a batch of 500 premium cards in the US or UK, expect:

- **Standard quality (350gsm matte):** $80–$150 USD
- **Premium soft-touch with spot UV:** $180–$350 USD
- **Luxury duplex or foil stamped:** $400–$800 USD

Per-card cost on a 500-unit run is $0.15–$1.60. For a firm closing $15,000 average projects, spending $300 on business cards that generate 10 meaningful follow-up conversations is a 50:1 ROI assumption — one that most marketing spend cannot match.

This falls within the broader [brand collateral checklist](/blog/brand-collateral-checklist) budget that every professional services firm should plan annually.

## How to Match Your Business Card to Your Brand Identity

Your business card should not be designed independently. It is one touchpoint within a broader identity system. Before you design or redesign your cards:

1. Confirm your brand colours have CMYK and Pantone equivalents (not just hex codes)
2. Verify your logo has a vector file at the correct resolution
3. Review your typography hierarchy so the card matches your other materials
4. Align finish choices with how your brand is positioned

If your brand is positioned as a premium firm, every [brand touchpoint](/blog/brand-touchpoints-guide) — including your business card — must reflect that positioning consistently. A premium website with a cheap business card creates cognitive dissonance that clients feel even if they cannot articulate it.
