Real estate is the highest-value transaction most people ever make.

They choose their agent carefully — and that decision process starts online, almost every time.

Your website is where a prospective seller decides whether you understand their neighbourhood, whether you've achieved prices like theirs, and whether they'd trust you to represent them.

A generic agency website — template design, stock photography, no local content — fails this test before the visitor reads a word.

Here's what to build instead.

---

## What Real Estate Clients Are Looking For Online

Before designing anything, understand what buyers and sellers actually look at on agent websites.

**Sellers want to know:**
- Have you sold properties like mine, in my area, at the prices I want?
- What is my property worth?
- Why should I choose you over the four other agents I'm evaluating?

**Buyers want to know:**
- Do you know this area?
- Can you find me something I haven't already seen on Rightmove or Zillow?
- Are you someone I can trust to negotiate on my behalf?

Your website needs to answer these questions — clearly, quickly, and credibly.

<Callout type="tip" title="Lead with Local Knowledge">
The most effective differentiator for independent real estate agents against larger firms is demonstrated local knowledge. A page showing your recent sales with actual prices, specific neighbourhood commentary, and local market data signals expertise that a national brand can't replicate.
</Callout>

---

## The 5 Pages Every Real Estate Agent Website Needs

### 1. Homepage

Your homepage has 10 seconds to establish credibility.

It needs: your name, your area, your track record (transactions completed, years active, average sale price), and a clear primary call to action — "Get a free valuation" or "Search properties."

Avoid: full-screen stock photography of anonymous houses, generic taglines, and three competing CTAs.

### 2. About Page

Real estate is personal. Clients hire you, not your firm.

Your about page should explain why you work in this area specifically, what your sales record looks like, what kind of clients you work with best, and what the experience of working with you is like.

Include a professional photo. Include specific numbers ("214 properties sold since 2018, average 4% above asking price in SW London"). Specific numbers outperform generic claims every time.

### 3. Local Area Pages

A dedicated page for each neighbourhood or area you serve is your highest-value SEO investment.

Each page should cover: typical property types, price ranges, market conditions, schools and amenities, and your specific record in that area.

These pages rank for "[area] estate agent" searches and demonstrate local expertise simultaneously. Read [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide) for the full approach.

### 4. Listings / Current Properties

Keep this simple and current. Feature your active listings with quality photography and clear pricing.

Don't integrate a full MLS or portal feed — it creates maintenance complexity and puts you in competition with portals you can't beat. Your own listings, curated and presented professionally, are more valuable.

### 5. Contact / Valuation

This is your primary conversion page.

Offer two paths: "Book a valuation" (for sellers) and "Talk to an agent" (for buyers). Both should have short forms — name, phone, email, property details.

Remove friction. Every extra field reduces conversion.

## Photography: The Fastest Trust Signal

Property photography quality signals agent quality.

If the photography on your website is poor, it suggests your property marketing will be poor. If it's excellent, it demonstrates the standard you'll apply when marketing a client's home.

Invest in professional photography for every listing you feature on your website. This investment pays for itself in the confidence it gives prospective sellers.

## Local SEO for Real Estate Agents

Local SEO is the highest-return marketing channel for most independent estate agents.

**The basics:**

- **Google Business Profile** — fully completed, with professional photos, regular posts, and active review collection. See [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide).
- **Area landing pages** — individual pages for every area you serve, optimised for "[area] estate agent" searches
- **Schema markup** — LocalBusiness schema and RealEstateAgent schema help search engines understand your business
- **Review strategy** — actively request reviews after every transaction; they drive both search visibility and conversion

For Bing visibility specifically: include your primary keyword ("estate agent in [area]" or "real estate agent [city]") in your page title, H1, and first paragraph on every area page.

## Mobile Design for Real Estate Websites

Over 65% of real estate searches happen on mobile.

A real estate website that isn't excellent on mobile is losing the majority of its potential leads.

Mobile essentials:
- **Click-to-call button** prominently placed — many buyers and sellers want to call immediately
- **Forms that work well on touch** — no tiny fields, clear labels, large submission buttons
- **Photography that loads fast** — compress property images aggressively
- **Simple navigation** — buyers and sellers should find your listing pages and contact form in two taps
