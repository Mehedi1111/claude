Aligning brand aesthetics on all social profiles is one of those tasks that seems minor until you actually check your own profiles. Most business owners are shocked when they look at their LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, and X side by side. The logo is different sizes. The bio says different things. The cover images are from three different eras of the business.

None of this is deliberate. It just accumulates over time as you update one platform but forget the others. The fix is straightforward. Here's how to do it properly.

---

## Why Does Brand Inconsistency Happen?

Because social media moves fast and brand management moves slow.

You design a new logo — and update your website, but forget LinkedIn. You write a better bio — and change your Instagram, but leave Facebook alone. You rebrand — and update the active platforms, but leave dormant ones untouched.

Two years later you have four different visual identities across four platforms. None of them are wrong exactly. But together they signal a brand that nobody's minding.

The platforms themselves don't help. Every platform has different image dimensions, different bio character limits, different ways of displaying your profile. What looks right on LinkedIn looks wrong on Instagram. So businesses adapt — and in adapting, diverge.

The solution isn't to make everything identical. It's to make everything coherent.

---

## What Needs to Be Consistent?

Not everything needs to match perfectly. These six elements do.

**1. Your logo or profile photo**

Use the same image across every platform. Not a different crop. Not an older version. The same file, sized correctly for each platform's current specifications.

For most service businesses, your profile photo should be your logo mark (symbol only, not full wordmark — it won't be readable at profile photo size) or a professional headshot if you're a personal brand. Choose one approach and stick to it across every platform.

**2. Your brand name spelling**

This sounds obvious. It isn't. "Evoke Studio," "EvokeStudio," "EVOKE," and "evoke.studio" are four different versions of the same name — and some businesses use all four depending on when each profile was set up.

Pick one version. Use it everywhere. Exactly.

**3. Your bio language**

You don't need identical bios — platforms have different character limits and audiences. But the core claim should be the same. What you do, who you do it for, what makes you different.

Write one master bio (150–200 words) that contains everything important. Then adapt it to each platform's limit without changing the substance. When you update it, update all platforms at the same time.

**4. Your colour palette**

Any graphics, banners, cover images, or posts using your brand colours should use the same colours across every platform. Keep your HEX codes saved somewhere accessible — not in your head.

If your brand is navy blue and white, your LinkedIn cover image, your Instagram highlights covers, your Facebook banner, and your Twitter/X header should all feel like they come from the same visual world.

**5. Your cover images or banners**

LinkedIn, Facebook, X, and YouTube all have cover image spaces. These are prime visual real estate that most businesses waste.

Your cover images don't need to be identical — but they should look like they come from the same brand. Same style, same colours, same general aesthetic. If you've updated one recently, check all of them. [Brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-what-they-include) that include social media specifications make this much easier to maintain over time.

**6. Your link**

Every platform with a bio link should point to the same place — usually your website homepage or a specific landing page. Don't send different platforms to different destinations unless you have a deliberate reason.

---

## Platform-by-Platform: What to Check

**LinkedIn**

- Profile photo: professional headshot or logo mark, high resolution
- Banner: branded cover image at 1584 × 396px
- Headline: matches your positioning — not just your job title
- About section: full bio with your core message
- Company page (if you have one): logo, banner, description all consistent with website

**Instagram**

- Profile photo: logo mark or headshot at 320 × 320px (displays as circle)
- Bio: 150 character limit — distil your core message
- Story highlights: branded covers in your colour palette
- Link in bio: current and correct

**Facebook**

- Profile or Page photo: 170 × 170px (displays as circle on desktop)
- Cover: 820 × 312px, branded
- About section: consistent with LinkedIn description

**X (formerly Twitter)**

- Profile photo: 400 × 400px
- Header: 1500 × 500px, branded
- Bio: 160 characters — sharp version of your positioning

**YouTube** (if relevant)

- Channel art: 2560 × 1440px
- Profile photo: same as other platforms
- Channel description: matches bio language

---

## How to Audit Your Profiles Right Now

Do this today. Takes 20 minutes.

1. Open each social platform where your business has a presence
2. Screenshot each profile page
3. Put all screenshots side by side
4. Check: same logo? Same name spelling? Same colour palette? Same bio substance? Cover images feel related?

The gaps will be obvious. Fix them one platform at a time, starting with the ones your prospects are most likely to check — usually LinkedIn first for B2B service businesses, Instagram for visual or consumer businesses.

---

## How to Stay Consistent Going Forward

The audit fixes the past. A simple system prevents future drift.

**Create a social media brand reference.** One page or shared document with: your exact brand name spelling, your profile photo file ready to use, your bio copy in long and short versions, your hex colour codes, links to your cover image files. Anyone updating your social profiles uses this document, not their memory.

**Set a quarterly brand check.** Every three months, spend 15 minutes checking your profiles match each other and your current website. Especially after a rebrand, a logo update, or a website refresh — these are the moments when inconsistency most often creeps in.

**Update all platforms at once.** When you change your bio, change it everywhere in the same sitting. When you update your logo, update every platform. It takes an extra 20 minutes now and saves an embarrassing audit in a year.

A [brand standards document](/blog/what-are-brand-standards) is the formal version of this — it covers social media alongside every other brand touchpoint. For service businesses doing their own social management, a lighter one-page version is usually enough. What matters is that the reference exists, that it's accessible, and that it actually gets used.
