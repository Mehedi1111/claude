Brand stationery is the category of physical branded materials — letterhead, envelopes, compliment slips, notepads, and branded document templates — that professional firms send, hand over, and file on behalf of clients. In the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, physical stationery still plays an active role in professional services. A branded letterhead on a contract, a branded envelope for a formal engagement letter, a compliment slip in a welcome package — these touchpoints reinforce your positioning every time they appear.

This guide covers what brand stationery every professional firm should have, the design standards that make it work, and why the investment pays off in client perception.

---

## What Is Brand Stationery and Why Does It Matter?

Brand stationery refers to the suite of branded physical documents a business uses in professional communication. It matters because physical materials carry a weight — literally and figuratively — that digital communications do not. When a client receives a signed contract on branded letterhead versus a generic Word document, those are two different brand experiences. The first reinforces that you are a serious, established firm. The second raises subtle doubt.

For professional services firms charging $5,000–$100,000+ per engagement, every material touchpoint contributes to the client's perception of whether you are worth what you charge. Stationery is part of how you signal that answer.

## What Should a Professional Services Brand Stationery Suite Include?

A complete stationery suite for a B2B professional services firm includes:

1. **Letterhead** — A4 (or US Letter) branded document template for formal correspondence
2. **Compliment slip** — A smaller branded card sent with documents or packages
3. **Envelopes** — Branded DL envelopes (for UK/AU) or #10 envelopes (for US) for outgoing post
4. **Notepads** — Branded notepads for client meetings, workshops, or gift sets
5. **Presentation folders** — Branded folders for proposals, reports, and welcome packages
6. **Report/document template** — A Word or Google Docs template for deliverables, proposals, and reports

Firms at the entry level need at minimum letterhead and a document template. Firms positioning above $20,000 per engagement should consider the full suite.

## How Should Letterhead Be Designed?

Letterhead design follows a clear hierarchy. The top section (header) carries your brand identity; the body is clean and designed for readability; the footer carries contact and legal information.

**Header elements:**
- Logo (positioned top-left or centred, depending on your brand identity system)
- Company name (if not included in the logo)
- Optional: a single decorative element aligned with your brand (a rule, a colour block, a pattern)

**Footer elements:**
- Company address
- Website URL
- Phone number
- Company registration number (required for UK limited companies, Australian PTY LTDs, and US LLCs in some states)
- Optional: email, LinkedIn URL

**Technical specifications:**
- A4: 210 × 297mm (UK, AU, Canada) or US Letter: 8.5 × 11 inches (US)
- Minimum margins: 20mm on all sides for printable area
- File format for print: PDF (CMYK, 300 DPI)
- File format for digital use: Word or Google Docs template with embedded fonts

Your [brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-what-they-include) should specify exactly how letterhead is to be used and who is authorised to create correspondence on it.

## What Are the Brand Standards for Envelopes?

Branded envelopes are often overlooked but carry real brand impact — particularly when a client receives a formal letter or signed document. In the UK and Australia, DL envelopes (110 × 220mm) are standard for A4 letters folded in thirds. In the US, the #10 envelope (4.125 × 9.5 inches) is the business standard.

Branded options include:
- **Printed brand colour exterior** — Your brand colour on the outer face signals a recognisable piece of mail
- **Logo printed on the flap** — Subtle but visible when the client opens the envelope
- **Return address printed in brand typography** — Replaces generic handwritten or printed return labels
- **Window envelopes** — For high-volume correspondence, a window shows the letterhead recipient address

White-label envelopes with a printed label are acceptable for small batches. For firms sending 50+ formal letters per month, pre-printed branded envelopes are worth the investment.

## Do Digital Businesses Still Need Physical Brand Stationery?

Yes — even primarily digital businesses benefit from physical stationery in specific contexts. A signed contract arriving on branded letterhead makes the engagement feel more serious. A handwritten note on a branded compliment slip in a client gift set is memorable. A branded notepad left in a meeting room says something about how you prepare for client interactions.

The question is not whether to have stationery, but which pieces to prioritise. For digital-first firms, the minimum is: letterhead (as a digital template), a branded document template, and a small run of compliment slips. These three pieces cover 90% of the situations where physical materials matter.

Review the full [brand collateral checklist](/blog/brand-collateral-checklist) to prioritise which physical materials your firm needs first.

## How Should Brand Stationery Relate to Your Other Brand Materials?

Brand stationery must be designed as part of the same visual identity system as your [business card](/blog/business-card-design-guide), [pitch deck](/blog/pitch-deck-branding-guide), and [invoice design](/blog/invoice-branding-guide). The same logo, the same typefaces, the same colour palette, the same tone — all applied consistently across every piece.

A common failure is designing each material separately — business cards from one designer, letterhead from another, website from a third — resulting in three variations of your brand identity with different logo versions, slightly different colour values, and inconsistent typography. The client's brain registers these inconsistencies even when they cannot name them.

Your [brand colours](/blog/brand-colors-guide) must have CMYK values (not just hex codes) to print accurately across all stationery. Pantone references ensure colour consistency even when different print vendors are used.

## How Much Does Branded Stationery Cost?

For a typical professional services firm in the US or UK:

- **Letterhead design (one-time):** $300–$800 USD
- **Letterhead print (500 sheets, 120gsm, full colour):** $120–$250 USD
- **Compliment slips (250 units):** $80–$150 USD
- **#10 / DL envelopes, printed (500 units):** $150–$300 USD
- **Branded notepad (25 pads, 50 sheets each):** $200–$400 USD

Total investment for a complete stationery suite: $850–$2,000 USD. For a firm closing $25,000+ engagements, this is a de minimis investment in brand credibility that pays back on the first properly closed deal.
