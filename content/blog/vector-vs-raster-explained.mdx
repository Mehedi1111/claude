If you've ever been asked by a printer, developer, or brand agency to "send the vector file" and didn't know what they meant — this is for you.

The vector vs. raster distinction is one of the most practically important concepts in brand design. It determines whether your logo can be used professionally, at scale, and across every medium that matters.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Short Version">
Raster files are made of pixels. They break when you scale them up. Vector files are made of math. They're infinitely scalable. Your brand logo needs to be a vector.
</Callout>

## What Is a Raster File?

A raster file stores images as a fixed grid of coloured squares called pixels. JPG, PNG, WEBP, and GIF are all raster formats. At their native resolution, raster images look sharp. When you scale them up, the pixels become visible — a phenomenon called "pixelation."

Every AI-generated image — Midjourney, DALL-E, Ideogram, Stable Diffusion — is a raster file.

## What Is a Vector File?

A vector file stores images as mathematical equations. Instead of "pixel (100,100) is black," it says "draw a circle with centre point X, radius Y, fill colour Z." Because the description is mathematical, the rendering engine calculates a perfectly crisp result at any output size — from a 16px favicon to a 20-metre banner.

SVG, AI (Adobe Illustrator), and EPS are vector formats.

## Why Does Brand Logo Design Need Vectors?

Professional use of a brand logo spans an enormous range of scales and contexts. Consider the typical deployment of a single brand mark:

- A **responsive website** may render your logo at 40px on mobile
- A **business card** renders it at roughly 150px wide
- A **PowerPoint slide** might need it at 300px
- A **banner stand** needs it at ~2000px equivalent
- A **vehicle wrap** might need it at 10,000px or larger
- A **building sign** might require it at 50,000px

No raster file survives this range with its integrity intact. A vector does — effortlessly, and with a file size often smaller than the PNG equivalent.

<Callout type="note">
The reason your developer or printer keeps asking for "the SVG" is not because of preference. It's because without it, they literally cannot do their job correctly.
</Callout>

## The AI Logo Problem

AI image generators are extraordinarily capable at producing visually compelling brand concepts. The problem is that their output format — raster — is fundamentally unsuitable for professional brand use.

This has created an entirely new category of design need: **AI logo vectorisation**. The process of manually reconstructing an AI-generated raster logo as a precise vector file.

The key word is manually. Auto-trace tools (such as Adobe Illustrator's Image Trace) exist, but they produce bloated, imprecise paths that fail professional use cases. True vectorisation requires a designer to study the raster output and rebuild every path, curve, and letterform from scratch.

## What You Should Always Have

For any brand logo, you need a minimum of:

| File | Format | Use Case |
|------|--------|----------|
| Master vector | `.ai` or `.eps` | Source of truth |
| Web SVG | `.svg` | Websites, apps |
| Print PDF | `.pdf` | Print production |
| Transparent PNG | `.png` at 3x | Social, email |
| White version | All of the above | Dark backgrounds |

If you don't have all of these — particularly the vector source files — your brand is not production-ready.
