A website redesign is among the most consequential digital marketing decisions a business makes. When executed correctly, a redesign increases organic traffic, improves conversion rates, reduces bounce rates, and positions the business more compellingly against competitors. When executed incorrectly, it destroys years of accumulated search authority, loses organic rankings that drive inbound leads, and alienates the audience you spent years attracting. In the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, businesses lose millions annually to botched website redesigns — most of which were entirely avoidable.

This guide covers when a website redesign is justified, how to protect your SEO during the process, what the redesign workflow involves, and how to avoid the most costly mistakes.

---

## When Does a Website Need a Redesign?

Not every website problem requires a redesign. The decision to redesign should be driven by evidence, not aesthetics. Signs that a redesign is genuinely warranted:

**Performance degradation:** Pages loading in over 3 seconds on mobile, Core Web Vitals failures (LCP over 4s, CLS above 0.1, INP over 500ms), Lighthouse scores under 50. These are technical debt problems that compound over time.

**Conversion rate decline:** A conversion rate below industry average for your sector (see [website conversion rate optimisation](/blog/website-conversion-rate-optimization) for benchmarks) or a trend of declining enquiries despite stable or growing traffic.

**Brand misalignment:** The website no longer reflects the business's positioning, pricing, or target audience. A common issue after a rebrand, an acquisition, or a significant market repositioning.

**Mobile failure:** A website built before 2018 that was never properly mobile-optimised. Over 60% of web traffic in the US and UK is mobile, and a non-responsive site is leaving that majority with a broken experience.

**Technology obsolescence:** A website built on technology that is no longer supported, cannot integrate with modern tools (CRMs, marketing automation, payment processors), or cannot be maintained without specialist legacy knowledge.

**Signs that a redesign is NOT warranted:** The CEO or marketing director personally dislikes the current design but the data shows good performance. A competitor launched a new website. The existing site is less than 2 years old. In these cases, targeted improvements will outperform a full redesign.

## What Are the Biggest Risks in a Website Redesign?

**SEO destruction:** The most common and most costly mistake. Changing URLs without proper 301 redirects causes every inbound link, every Google-indexed page, and every bookmarked URL to return a 404 error. Practices with 500 inbound links to specific pages have lost 60–80% of their organic traffic overnight from URL structure changes without redirects.

**Content loss:** Redesigns frequently result in content being consolidated, shortened, or removed entirely in the name of "simplifying" the site. If that content was driving organic traffic, the traffic disappears with it.

**Speed regression:** Visually impressive new designs that load in 7 seconds perform worse than the "outdated" site they replaced. Modern design ambition must be paired with performance engineering.

**Conversion regression:** A site that looked old but converted at 4.5% was more valuable than its beautiful replacement converting at 2.1%. Never redesign without establishing the current conversion baseline first.

## How Should SEO Be Protected During a Website Redesign?

SEO protection during a redesign requires four actions:

**1. Crawl and document all existing URLs.** Before touching anything, use Screaming Frog or Sitebulb to crawl the existing site and export every URL that returns a 200 status code. This is the master list of pages that need to be accounted for in the new site.

**2. Map 301 redirects for every changed URL.** If any URL changes in the new site, create a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new equivalent. This is not optional — it is the most important SEO task in any redesign.

**3. Preserve high-value content.** Identify which pages drive the most organic traffic using Google Search Console. These pages must exist in the new site with equivalent or better content. Do not consolidate a 1,200-word page that drives 3,000 monthly visits into a 200-word summary.

**4. Verify the new site before launch.** Check all redirects function correctly. Verify canonical tags. Confirm robots.txt does not block indexing. Submit the new sitemap to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools within 24 hours of launch.

## What Does the Website Redesign Process Involve?

A professional website redesign follows seven stages:

**Stage 1: Discovery and audit (2–3 weeks)**
Analytics audit, conversion analysis, SEO audit, competitor research, stakeholder interviews, and content inventory. Establish the baseline performance metrics the new site must beat.

**Stage 2: Strategy and positioning (1–2 weeks)**
Define the site's primary conversion goal, target audience, and positioning statement. This stage resolves the "what are we trying to achieve" question before any design begins.

**Stage 3: Information architecture (1 week)**
Site structure, page hierarchy, URL structure, and navigation design. Changes made here determine the SEO and UX of the entire site.

**Stage 4: Design (3–5 weeks)**
Wireframes, design system, page templates, and high-fidelity designs for core pages. See [web design for professional services](/blog/web-design-for-professional-services) for design standards by industry.

**Stage 5: Development (4–8 weeks)**
Build on the chosen platform (Next.js, Webflow, WordPress). Includes performance optimisation, accessibility implementation, and CMS configuration.

**Stage 6: Content migration (1–3 weeks)**
Content is migrated, redirects are configured, and SEO metadata is verified for all pages.

**Stage 7: QA and launch (1–2 weeks)**
Cross-device testing, performance testing, redirect verification, and staged launch.

## How Long Does a Website Redesign Take?

A professional website redesign for a business with 20–50 pages takes 12–18 weeks from strategy to launch. Expedited timelines are possible for smaller sites (8–10 weeks for sites under 10 pages) but typically sacrifice quality or thoroughness in the discovery and SEO protection stages.

Attempting to compress a redesign into 4–6 weeks consistently produces the two most expensive outcomes: poor SEO protection and a live site that fails performance benchmarks.

## How Much Does a Website Redesign Cost?

A professionally managed website redesign in the US, UK, Canada, or Australia:

- **Small business (under 15 pages):** $5,000–$12,000 USD
- **Professional services (15–40 pages):** $10,000–$25,000 USD
- **Ecommerce (50–200 products):** $12,000–$35,000 USD
- **Enterprise (100+ pages, complex functionality):** $30,000–$100,000+ USD
