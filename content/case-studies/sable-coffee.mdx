When Sable Coffee's first batch of branded cups arrived from the printer, head roaster Marcus Teo knew immediately something was wrong. The deep, warm espresso brown he'd seen on screen — a colour that had taken forty-two Midjourney and DALL-E 3 iterations to perfect — had printed as a burnt orange.

Not close. Not approximately right. A different colour entirely.

## What Went Wrong

DALL-E 3 generates images in RGB colour space. RGB is designed for screens, which emit light in red, green, and blue channels. Print uses CMYK — cyan, magenta, yellow, and black pigments that absorb light rather than emit it. The conversion between these two colour systems is not automatic, and without explicit colour management, the result is unpredictable.

Sable's deep espresso brown was specified as `R:62 G:29 B:13` in the original DALL-E output. Under unmanaged CMYK conversion, this rendered as approximately C:15 M:68 Y:78 K:45 on the printer's press — which produces a warm orange-brown that bore no resemblance to the intended colour.

<Callout type="warning" title="The Colour Math Problem">
RGB `#3E1D0D` — Sable's intended espresso brown — converts to CMYK differently depending on which ICC profile is used, the substrate, and the printer's calibration. Without an explicit Pantone reference, every vendor will apply their own interpretation. The result: four different browns across four different vendors.
</Callout>

The cups weren't the only casualty. Sable's kraft paper coffee bags — from a different vendor — had also run with the orange interpretation. 12,000 units of packaging that could not be used.

## The Audit

We received the original DALL-E 3 export and ran a full technical audit before touching any paths.

| Issue Found | Impact |
|------------|--------|
| Unmanaged RGB colour (no ICC profile) | Colour shift at every print vendor |
| 4 open paths in the mark | Unpredictable fill behaviour |
| 2 strokes not converted to outlines | Weight inconsistency at small sizes |
| No Pantone equivalent specified | Impossible to proof consistently |
| Missing bleed and crop marks | Edge artefacts in packaging die-cut |

The mark itself — a minimal geometric coffee bean form with a subtle wordmark — was visually strong. The problems were entirely technical, not creative.

## The Colour Correction Process

Identifying the correct Pantone equivalent for a rich, warm dark brown required print testing, not just profile conversion. We tested three Pantone candidates against the client's intended colour on both the coated paper cup stock and the uncoated kraft bag material before specifying the final values.

The final Sable colour system:
- **Pantone:** 4625 C (coated) / 4625 U (uncoated)
- **CMYK:** C20 M68 Y78 K55
- **RGB:** R87 G42 B24
- **Hex:** #572A18

These values are now documented in Sable's brand file set, so every future vendor — packaging, signage, stationery — receives an unambiguous specification.

## The Outcome

The corrected files were accepted by both vendors on the first submission. The replacement cup run completed in time for Sable's café opening. The kraft bag stock was similarly approved. Sable Coffee now operates with a single documented colour specification that produces consistent results across all production contexts.

Within three months of opening, Sable was stocked as a wholesale account by three additional specialty coffee venues — all of which required brand files for their own menu and signage use. Every file was delivered from the same clean, print-certified source.
