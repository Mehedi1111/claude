Callum & Vance Solicitors had practiced family and commercial law in their region for twenty-five years. Their anniversary rebrand was the first time the firm had invested in premium branded stationery — letterpress business cards and embossed letterhead, ordered from a specialist printer known for producing materials for the region's most established professional firms.

The printer rejected the file three times before Callum & Vance came to us.

## The Auto-Trace Problem

The firm's office manager had asked a local freelancer to "sort out the logo file" after the first rejection. The freelancer had opened the logo PNG in Adobe Illustrator, run Image Trace, expanded the result, and delivered a 47MB Illustrator file.

On screen, it looked identical to the original.

The printer saw something different: a file with 14,800 anchor points, inconsistent stroke weights, and paths that created rendering conflicts in their specialised letterpress RIP software. The embossing vendor rejected it for the same reasons — the redundant complexity made it impossible to generate a clean deboss plate.

<Callout type="warning" title="Why Auto-Trace Fails for Premium Print Processes">
Letterpress and embossing work from physical plates machined from digital path data. Thousands of redundant anchor points produce plates with microscopic inconsistencies. What looks clean at screen resolution — even at 300 DPI — breaks down when translated to physical tooling at micron tolerances. Premium print processes require genuinely minimal, precise vector paths.
</Callout>

## The Audit

We received the auto-traced file alongside the original PNG and ran a complete technical audit.

| Issue | Auto-Traced File | Our Reconstruction |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Anchor points | 14,847 | 186 |
| Stroke-to-path conversion | Not completed | All strokes outlined |
| Proportion consistency | Optical irregularities | Grid-corrected |
| Spot colour isolation | Not present | Separated |
| Emboss compatibility | Failed RIP | Passed |

The Callum & Vance mark — a traditional shield form with serif wordmark — had been in use for twenty-five years. Our brief was not to redesign it, but to deliver a technically perfect vector version of the mark the firm had always had. Every proportion correction was made to serve accuracy, not aesthetics.

## The Reconstruction

The shield form was rebuilt on a geometric grid derived from the original proportions. The serif letterforms in the wordmark were reconstructed as custom vector paths — the typeface used in the original was a traditional legal face not available as a licensed vector font, so each character was hand-drawn to match.

The final file was delivered with two clearly labelled layers:
1. **Primary colour layer** — the main mark in Process Black (K100)
2. **Spot foil layer** — a secondary gold element isolated as Pantone 872 C

This structure allowed the letterpress printer to pull each colour as a separate plate — exactly what the process required.

## The Brand Standards Document

As part of the engagement, we delivered a concise brand standards document covering the firm's corrected logo files: approved colour values (CMYK, Pantone, RGB, hex), minimum size specifications, clear-space rules, and usage guidance for future vendors. The document ensures that any future printer or designer the firm engages receives unambiguous specifications.

The anniversary stationery launched on schedule. Both the letterpress business cards and the embossed letterhead were received to the standard the firm had invested in.
