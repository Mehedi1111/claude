Volta Payments builds B2B payment infrastructure for financial institutions. Their technology — a ledger reconciliation layer that reduces settlement time for mid-market banks — was technically mature. Their commercial traction was real. Their brand was a Midjourney mark that a member of the founding team had generated in an evening.

With three term sheet meetings scheduled in six weeks, the founding team knew the brand needed to change. Not because the mark was wrong — it was a clean geometric lightning bolt form that communicated speed and precision — but because the brand system around it was nonexistent. The pitch deck used three different typefaces. The website and the one-pager used different logo versions. There were no colour values, no brand rules, and no files that a journalist or designer could work with.

## The Investor Problem

Institutional investors see hundreds of decks. They make rapid quality judgements. A mismatched typeface on slide two signals something — not consciously, but the impression accumulates. A brand that looks like a weekend placeholder communicates that the team hasn't thought carefully about market positioning, audience perception, or the value of precision.

Volta's product was built on precision. The brand needed to say the same thing.

<Callout type="note" title="What Investors Actually See">
Before reading a deck, investors register the quality of it. Typography consistency, colour discipline, layout precision, and the professionalism of the logo mark all communicate before a single word is processed. A brand that looks like a $150,000 company will be evaluated like one.
</Callout>

## The Brand System

We had eight days. The approach was structured and deliberate.

**Day 1: Mark Vectorisation**
The Midjourney lightning bolt mark was rebuilt as a precise vector. The geometric form — two angular planes creating the bolt's characteristic bend — was reconstructed on a mathematical grid. One subtle correction: the original Midjourney output had a 3° asymmetry in the upper plane that was imperceptible in isolation but would have been visible in applications with precise symmetry requirements (embossed stationery, large-format print).

**Days 2–3: Brand System Construction**
Volta's positioning required a brand that communicated speed, precision, and institutional credibility. We established:

- **2-colour system:** Finance Black (`#0A0A0A`) and Electric Blue (Pantone 2728 C) — a blue with the brightness to signal technology and the depth to signal security
- **Typography:** Inter for all applications — legible, precise, and widely available to any team member or vendor
- **Grid:** 8-column structure with defined spacing ratios, used across all brand applications

**Days 4–5: Pitch Deck Template**
A 24-slide master template in Figma and PowerPoint: cover, team, problem, solution, traction, market, ask — all pages designed to the brand system, with placeholder text and image areas. The team could populate content without making any design decisions.

**Days 6–7: Press Kit and Brand Guidelines**
- A 20-page brand guidelines PDF
- Press kit: logo files in all formats, founder photography guidance, approved copy for "About the company"
- All logo files in SVG, PNG, and PDF for journalist and media use

## The Outcome

All three term sheet meetings progressed to due diligence. Volta Payments closed a $4.2M Series A. At launch, TechCrunch covered the raise — the brand assets in the press kit were used directly in the article, confirming they met editorial publication standards.

The pitch deck template is still in active use. The brand system has been extended by Volta's in-house designer to their product interface and marketing materials without any additional direction from us — the guidelines were clear enough to operate independently.
